**Selected Beachhead:** Large metros (500K-2M population) in Türkiye with established digital infrastructure but fragmented citizen engagement tools.

## Selected Beachhead Market with Reasoning

**Target:** İzmir, Antalya, Eskişehir as initial pilot cities

**Reasoning:**
1. Population size sufficient for meaningful data (750K-1.5M each)
2. Existing but fragmented digital services create whitespace
3. Known for innovation-forward leadership (especially İzmir and Eskişehir)
4. Tourism economy in Antalya creates visible ROI pressure
5. Cultural readiness for civic engagement apps
6. Geographic spread provides regional diversification
7. Mid-size creates balance between volume and manageability

**Rejection of Istanbul/Ankara:** Too competitive, bureaucratic complexity slows adoption, market already saturated with civic apps.

**Rejection of smaller cities:** Not enough volume for sustainable growth, digital literacy too variable, municipalities lack innovation budgets.

## End User Profile

### Primary: The Frustrated Urban Resident

**Demographics:**
- Age: 25-50
- Income: Middle class ( ₺25,000-75,000/month)
- Location: Urban residential neighborhoods
- Education: High school or higher
- Tech: Daily smartphone user, social media active

**Behaviors:**
- Regularly encounters infrastructure issues in daily routine
- Has tried reporting through at least one channel (app, hotline, social media)
- Has experienced no follow-up or response from past reports
- Currently uses social media to complain about civic issues
- Willing to download app if clear value proposition

**Pain Points:**
- "I report but nothing happens"
- "I don't know if my report was even received"
- "I waste time reporting issues that never get fixed"
- "There's no way to know if anyone actually cares"
- "I feel like my voice doesn't matter"

**Jobs to Be Done:**
1. Report an issue with minimal friction (photo + location + category)
2. See that others care about the same issues (social proof)
3. Track progress without asking (transparency)
4. Know when issues are resolved (closure)
5. Feel like my civic participation matters (validation)

|--------------|-------------|-----------|------------|

### Substitute Behaviors
1. **Do nothing:** Accept issues as part of city living (most common)
2. **Social media shaming:** Viral posts to create pressure (reactive)
3. **Direct contact:** Personal connections to municipal staff (privilege-based)
4. **Media involvement:** Contact local news for visibility (extreme cases)

**Key Insight:** Most citizens currently choose "do nothing" because existing alternatives don't deliver tangible results. VisibleFix wins by delivering on the transparency promise that others make but don't keep.

## User Life Cycle Use Case

### Lifecycle Stage 1: Discovery
- **Trigger:** Community discussion about infrastructure issues, social media post about app, neighborhood association mention
- **Action:** Download app / visit website
- **Success:** Complete registration (or anonymous), understand value proposition
- **Failure:** Confused by UI, leave without understanding value

### Lifecycle Stage 2: First Report
- **Trigger:** Encounters issue, remembers app
- **Action:** Open app → Take photo → Auto-location → Submit
- **Success:** Report submitted, appears on map
- **Failure:** Camera/permissions denied, location not captured, submission fails

### Lifecycle Stage 3: Engagement Loop
- **Trigger:** Notification or curiosity about reported issue
- **Action:** Return to app, check status, see other issues
- **Success:** See issue acknowledged, upvote other issues, share with friends
- **Failure:** No status change after 1+ week, no other engagement, app deleted

### Lifecycle Stage 4: Habit
- **Trigger:** Regular usage, sees value
- **Action:** Proactively report, check map regularly, engage with community
- **Success:** Becomes regular user, contributes to data quality, advocates for platform
- **Failure:** Stops using after initial novelty, limited engagement

### Lifecycle Stage 5: Advocate
- **Trigger:** Issue resolved, positive experience
- **Action:** Share on social media, recommend to neighbors, submit improvement feedback
- **Success:** Organic growth through word-of-mouth, community organizing around platform
- **Failure:** Neutral or negative experience prevents advocacy

## High-Level TAM / SAM / SOM Logic

### Total Addressable Market (TAM)
- **Definition:** All municipalities in Türkiye + comparable middle-income countries
- **Calculation:** 1,397 municipalities in Türkiye × average annual civic tech budget (₺5M) = ~₺7B
- **Global extension:** 30+ comparable countries × similar calculations = $15-20B theoretical market

### Serviceable Addressable Market (SAM)
- **Definition:** Municipalities in Türkiye with 500K-2M population + pilot countries
- **Calculation:** ~50 cities × ₺5M average = ₺250M
- **Reality check:** ~2% adoption in 5 years = ₺5M annual revenue potential

### Serviceable Obtainable Market (SOM)
- **Definition:** Active users in pilot cities + paid municipal features
- **Calculation:** 
  - Pilot cities: 3 × 750K population × 5% active users = 112,500 users
  - Paid features: 3 cities × ₺500K/year = ₺1.5M ARR
- **3-year target:** 15 cities × ₺2M = ₺30M ARR

### Revenue Model
1. **Free tier:** Public issue reporting + map + status tracking (ad-supported or municipal funding)
2. **Standard (₺50K/month):** Municipal dashboard, analytics, issue management
3. **Premium (₺150K/month):** Custom branding, API access, advanced analytics, priority support
4. **Enterprise:** White-label, dedicated infrastructure, SLA guarantees

## Sequencing of Experiments to Validate Assumptions

### Phase 1: Desirability Validation (Weeks 1-3)

**Experiment 1.1: Pain Survey**
- Method: Online survey (n=500) in pilot cities
- Hypothesis: >60% of respondents report issues monthly, >70% say nothing happens
- Success criteria: Pain validated quantitatively

**Experiment 1.2: Value Prop Test**
- Method: Landing page with animated mockup, email capture
- Hypothesis: >15% visitor-to-signup rate for waitlist
- Success criteria: Interest validated before building

**Experiment 1.3: Channel Audit**
- Method: Document all reporting channels in 3 pilot cities
- Hypothesis: Fragmentation creates whitespace
- Success criteria: Clear differentiation identified

### Phase 2: Feasibility Validation (Weeks 4-6)

**Experiment 2.1: Reporting UX Test**
- Method: Clickable prototype with 10 users
- Hypothesis: <2 minutes to submit first report
- Success criteria: Usability validated

**Experiment 2.2: Map Visualization Test**
- Method: Display dummy data on map, user feedback
- Hypothesis: Map view preferred over list for discovery
- Success criteria: UI preference validated

**Experiment 2.3: Status System Test**
- Method: Simulate status changes, user interviews
- Hypothesis: Status updates create perceived accountability
- Success criteria: Concept resonates

### Phase 3: Business Model Validation (Weeks 7-10)

**Experiment 3.1: Municipal Interview**
- Method: 5 in-depth interviews with innovation/municipal staff
- Hypothesis: Interest in pilot program with specific triggers
- Success criteria: Pipeline of qualified prospects

**Experiment 3.2: Willingness to Pay**
- Method: Pricing survey with scenario-based questions
- Hypothesis: >25% indicate willingness to pay for features
- Success criteria: Business model viability signal

**Experiment 3.3: Community Partner Interviews**
- Method: Interviews with neighborhood associations, NGOs
- Hypothesis: Willingness to organize and promote
- Success criteria: Distribution channel identified

### Phase 4: MVP Launch (Weeks 11-14)

**Experiment 4.1: Beta Launch**
- Method: Limited release in one neighborhood
- Hypothesis: Organic adoption from community seeding
- Success criteria: >500 reports in first month, retention >40%

**Experiment 4.2: Iteration Based on Feedback**
- Method: Continuous improvement based on user feedback
- Hypothesis: Iterations improve satisfaction
- Success criteria: NPS improvement >20 points
